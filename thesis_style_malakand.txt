Initally three copies of ring binded thesis should be submitted by the candidate for

evaluation and will become the property of University of Malakand. Text throughout the

document should be in times new roman, font size 12 and line spacing 1.5. The size of the

typing paper should be A4 with 1.5 inch margins to the left and 1 inch margins for the rest of

three sides. All pages except the title page must be numbered at least 3/4" from the bottom

of the page toward the right side. Use small Roman numerals for all preliminary pages. Begin

the numbering with a small Roman numeral ii at least 3/4" from the bottom of the page

toward the right side, following the title page. Use Arabic numerals beginning with the

Introduction or Chapter I of the main text of the thesis, and continue with every sheet that

follows, whether it be text, figures, explanation for figures or photos, tables, maps,

appendices, etc., numbering pages to the end.

The final five copies of the thesis must be hard bound in maroon color for PhD and black

color for MPhil. The complete title of the thesis should be printed in block, golden letters at

the top of the cover. In the middle of the cover, full name of the candidate should be

printed. The spine should have the surname of the scholar, the year in which the thesis is

presented (Sample of the title pages are given in Annex-1)

Composition of Thesis

The thesis shall preferably contain the following parts. However; the numbers of chapters

may increase or decrease by the faculties/departments/centers/institutes, in the light of the

subject or nature of the study.

                  1. Abstract

                  2. Introduction

                  3. Experimental /Material and Methods

                  4. Results and Discussions
                   5. Conclusion

                   6. References

Subdivision - numbered sections

Thesis should be clearly divided into defined and numbered sections. Subsections should be

numbered 1.1 (then 1.1.1, 1.1.2, ...), 1.2, etc. (the abstract will not included in section

numbering). Use this numbering also for internal cross-referencing: do not just refer to 'the

text'. Any subsection may be given a brief heading. Each heading should appear on its own

separate line.

Abstract

Abstract should be concise and factual. The abstract should state briefly the purpose of the

research, the principal results and major conclusions. An abstract is often presented

separately from the thesis, so it must be able to stand alone. For this reason, references

should be avoided, but if essential, they must be cited in full, without reference to the

reference list. Non-standard or uncommon abbreviations should be avoided, but if essential

they must be defined at their first mention in the abstract itself.

Introduction

Introduction should be arranged by each scholar as per his/her Departmental criterion.

Where the scholar will state the objectives of the work and provide an adequate

background, adding a detailed literature review as a separate subsection. References should

clearly be cited in the text following proper sequence and the scholar is supposed to arrange

them at the end of his/her thesis. Indicate references by number(s) in brackets in line with

the text. The actual authors can be referred to, but the reference number(s) must always be

given.
Experimental or Material and Methods

Provide sufficient detail to allow the work to be reproduced. Methods already published

should be indicated by a reference: only relevant modifications should be described.

Results and Discussions

There should be combined Results and Discussion section. Results should be clear and

concise. And discussion should explore the significance of the results of the work. Avoid

extensive citations and discussion of unpublished literature.

Conclusions

The main conclusions of the study may be presented in a conclusions section, which may

stand alone or form a subsection of Results and Discussion section.

References

For references should contain all authors, title of the document, name of the publishing

company/Journal, volume number, issue number, pagination and year in which it appears.

Literature should include recent references as much as possible. Specimen references are

given below.

   1.      Thesis

           Author’s name, title of the thesis in inverted commas, degree granting University,

           location of the university, date of completion in bold, ie,

           U. A. Khan, “Title of the thesis” PhD dissertation, University of Malakand,

           Chakdara, 2012.

   2.    Patents

           Patent owner(s) separated by comma, “title of the patent” patent number, date

           in bold. i.e.,
       S. K. Sheem, “low-cost fibre optic pressure sensor” US patent No. 4,544,587,

       March 20, 2011.

3. Citation of a Journal

       Authors separated by commas if more than one, title of publication in inverted

       commas, name of the Journal in abbreviation and italic, Volume (Issue),

       Pagination (year). i.e.

       M. Sadiq, K. Saeed, “Biological applications of ABC” Am. J. Chem. 45(2), 1230-

       1232 (2012).

4. Meetings and conferences

       Author 1, Author 2, etc “Title of presentation” Name of the meetings, Location of

       meeting, Date of meeting; Editor 1, Editor 2, etc., Eds.; Publisher: place of

       publication, Abstract No, pagination, (Year). i.e,

       E. Garrone, P. Uhl “Structure and reactivity of surfaces” 11 th international

       conference of material sciences, University of Peshawar, September 11-14, 2012;

       A. Zecchina, G. Cost, Eds.; Wiley Interscience, New York (2013).


5. Government publications

       Author 1, Author 2 etc. “Document title Document” Government publication

       number if any, publishing agency, place of publication, pagination (year) etc,

       R. G. Thomas, S. E. Gebhardt “Nutritive values of food” Home and Garden Bulletin

       No. 72, Department of Agriculture, Pakistan Government press, Islamabad (2009).

6. Web sites

       Author (if any), “Title of site” URL (accessed Month Day, Year), other identifying

       information (if any). i.e.,

       ACS publication division home page. http://pubs.acs.org (accessed Nov. 7, 2007)
7. Book

     Author, “Title of the book” (Editors if any, in parenthesis) Edition, Publishing

     company, Location, Year of Publishing. i.e.,

     L. Brandsma, “Synthesis of Acetylenes, Allenes, and Cumulenes – Methods and

     Techniques” 1st Edition, Elsevier, Amsterdam, 2004.
